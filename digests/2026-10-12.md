### 1. Comparison of antimicrobial susceptibility testing methods for aztreonam-avibactam against NDM-producing Enterobacterales clinical isolates.

- **Authors:** Rivas L, Díaz L, Quiroz V, et al.
- **Journal / Venue:** J Clin Microbiol
- **Date:** 2026-10-12
- **DOI:** [10.1128/jcm.00797-26](https://doi.org/10.1128/jcm.00797-26)
- **PMID:** [42861991](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42861991/)
- **Topics:** Antimicrobial Resistance

> Aztreonam-avibactam (ATM-AVI) is a promising therapeutic for New Delhi metallo-β-lactamase-producing Enterobacterales (NDM-E) infections. We evaluated the performance of disk diffusion and gradient diffusion methods compared with reference broth microdilution (BMD) for ATM-AVI susceptibility testing. A total of 245 non-duplicate NDM-E isolates were included, collected from unique patients at hospitals in Chile and the United States between 2022 and 2024. Nine ATM-AVI non-susceptible isolates from a separate collection were added to enrich for resistant isolates, yielding a total of 254 isolates. Reference BMD was performed according to Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI) criteria. ATM-AVI disk diffusion and two gradient diffusion methods (ETEST and MIC Test Strip) were evaluated against BMD using categorical agreement, essential agreement, and CLSI-defined error rates. All 245 NDM-E isolates were susceptible to ATM-AVI (MIC ≤4/4 µg/mL), with MIC₅₀/₉₀ values of 0.25/4 and 1/4 µg/mL, respectively. The collection consisted of Klebsiella pneumoniae (71%), Enterobacter cloacae complex (21%), and Escherichia coli (8%). Predominant NDM alleles included blaNDM-1 and blaNDM-7. Disk diffusion demonstrated 98% categorical agreement (250/254), with four minor errors. Both gradient diffusion methods achieved 99% categorical agreement (252/254), with no very major or major errors and two minor errors. Essential agreement was 67% for ETEST and 85% for MIC Test Strip; both methods tended to underestimate ATM-AVI MICs relative to BMD. ATM-AVI demonstrated robust in vitro activity against a contemporary NDM-E cohort. Disk and gradient diffusion methods are practical alternatives to BMD for clinical laboratories.

### 2. Toward routine implementation of phage testing: an inter-laboratory comparison of a disk-based susceptibility testing (Disk-PST).

- **Authors:** Ekat K, Scharnagl L, Hahn A, et al.
- **Journal / Venue:** J Clin Microbiol
- **Date:** 2026-10-12
- **DOI:** [10.1128/jcm.00029-26](https://doi.org/10.1128/jcm.00029-26)
- **PMID:** [42861955](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42861955/)
- **Topics:** Antimicrobial Resistance

> In the context of an emerging post-antibiotic era, phage therapy is gaining increasing attention. Standardized approaches for phage susceptibility testing (PST) are crucial to enable objective and effective phage selection for personalized treatment, yet no standardized reference method currently exists. To overcome this impediment, a standardized disk-based PST protocol was validated in-house (intra-laboratory comparison) involving 28 participants, followed by an inter-laboratory comparison across 12 laboratories in Germany with 39 participants. Preselection testing revealed high concordance between Disk-PST and Spot test, supporting the selection of strains with consistent phage susceptibility profiles for the interlaboratory comparison. An Escherichia coli phage, a Staphylococcus epidermidis phage, and a negative control (SM buffer) were tested on four bacterial strains (two E. coli and two S. epidermidis strains). Testing was blinded and included two technical and two biological replicates per condition. The plaque assay was performed at the coordinating laboratory (University Medical Center Rostock) and served as the reference standard. The in-house validation showed high reliability and strong agreement with the plaque assay, requiring only minor methodological adjustments prior to the inter-laboratory trial. The inter-laboratory comparison confirmed the robustness of the method, showing almost perfect agreement with the plaque assay across all participating laboratories. In the rare event of false-positive or inconclusive results, phage cross-contamination, misinterpretation of microcolonies within lysis zones, and limited information on negative controls due to the blinded test setting were causative. In accordance with its high inter-laboratory reliability, the PST-disk method seems to be highly suitable for easy implementation in routine laboratory settings. Further investigations are necessary to correlate phage test results with clinical therapy outcomes-an issue that should be addressed in future clinical trials.IMPORTANCEThe rapid global spread of multidrug-resistant bacteria has intensified the need for effective alternative therapeutic strategies, including bacteriophage therapy. Because phage activity is highly strain-specific, accurate and standardized phage susceptibility testing (PST) is essential for safe and effective clinical implementation, yet standardized protocols are currently lacking. In this nationwide, blinded inter-laboratory study, we evaluated a routine-diagnostic-adapted single-disk PST method and demonstrated its excellent agreement with the gold-standard double agar layer plaque assay. Importantly, this approach represents a significant improvement over existing PST methods by providing a standardized, reproducible, and easy-to-implement assay with high potential for widespread application in clinical routine microbiology laboratories.

### 3. Deep learning recognises antibiotic modes of action from brightfield images.

- **Authors:** Krentzel D, Kho K, Petit J, et al.
- **Journal / Venue:** Nat Commun
- **Date:** 2026-09-08
- **DOI:** [10.1038/s41467-026-76355-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76355-0)
- **PMID:** [42860392](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42860392/)
- **Topics:** Antimicrobial Resistance

> The antimicrobial resistance crisis urgently calls for antibiotics with novel modes of action (MoAs). While growth inhibition assays can identify antibiotic molecules, they miss promising compounds below inhibitory concentrations and cannot reveal their MoA. Imaging-based profiling of drug-treated bacteria can inform on MoA, but current approaches generally require fluorescent labelling and/or inhibitory concentrations and it remains unclear whether compounds with novel MoAs can be robustly detected. Here, we demonstrate a deep learning approach to recognise antibiotic MoAs from unlabelled images. We train a convolutional neural network to predict treatment conditions from brightfield images of Escherichia coli exposed to antibiotics covering eight MoAs. Our approach can detect drug exposure at subinhibitory concentrations and allows near-perfect MoA recognition, even when trained on only eight images per treatment condition. Previously unseen compounds are assigned to their correct MoA with good accuracy if the MoA is represented in the training data, and our method can robustly detect MoA novelty in five out of six considered MoAs, enabling microscopy-based identification of new antibiotic classes. We also achieve near-perfect MoA recognition in Klebsiella pneumoniae, suggesting applicability to other species. Our approach complements growth inhibition assays and is poised to improve the search for innovative antibiotic compounds.

### 4. Postoperative Candida auris Bloodstream Infection Following Cardiac Surgery: Diagnostic Delay and Serial Changes in Azole MIC Values: A Case Report.

- **Authors:** Khalid M, Li X, Lei L, et al.
- **Journal / Venue:** Infect Drug Resist
- **Date:** 2026-10-06
- **DOI:** [10.2147/IDR.S625639](https://doi.org/10.2147/IDR.S625639)
- **PMID:** [42859695](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42859695/) | **PMCID:** [PMC13653592](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC13653592/)
- **Topics:** Antimicrobial Resistance

> BACKGROUND: Candida auris is an emerging multidrug-resistant fungal pathogen associated with healthcare-associated outbreaks and invasive infections, particularly in critically ill patients. Early recognition remains challenging due to nonspecific clinical presentations and overlap with bacterial infections. Postoperative bloodstream infection due to C. auris, especially following cardiac surgery, is still relatively uncommon and may be underrecognized in clinical practice.
> 
> CASE PRESENTATION: A 69-year-old man developed persistent fever, dyspnea, and bilateral postoperative pulmonary infiltrates after modified Morrow surgery and bioprosthetic mitral valve replacement. The pulmonary findings were clinically diagnosed as severe pneumonia, although imaging was nonspecific and also raised the possibility of pulmonary edema or heart failure. Despite broad-spectrum anti-infective therapy, his condition did not improve. Three separate catheter-drawn blood cultures collected on 21, 24, and 28 July 2025 subsequently yielded Candida auris, confirming candidemia. No respiratory specimen yielded C. auris; therefore, the pulmonary abnormalities were considered concurrent with, rather than microbiologically attributable to, the bloodstream infection. Antifungal susceptibility testing showed persistently high fluconazole MICs, while itraconazole and voriconazole MICs increased in the third isolate. Because validated C. auris-specific clinical breakpoints were unavailable for these agents, the MIC findings were interpreted descriptively. Following adjustment of antifungal therapy and continued supportive care, the patient became afebrile, achieved clinical stabilization, and had a negative follow-up blood culture before discharge.
> 
> CONCLUSION: This case highlights the diagnostic challenge of Candida auris bloodstream infection in postoperative patients, where clinical features may mimic bacterial pneumonia. Persistent fever unresponsive to empirical antimicrobial therapy should prompt early consideration of invasive fungal infection. Timely microbiological diagnosis and appropriate antifungal treatment are essential for improving outcomes. Increased awareness of C. auris infection in surgical and hospitalized patients is critical for early recognition and effective management.

### 5. Clinical and Economic Outcomes Associated with Rapid Antimicrobial Susceptibility Reporting and Dual-Card Testing in ICU Bloodstream Infections.

- **Authors:** Xu Y, Feng Y, Shi C, et al.
- **Journal / Venue:** Infect Drug Resist
- **Date:** 2026-10-06
- **DOI:** [10.2147/IDR.S632364](https://doi.org/10.2147/IDR.S632364)
- **PMID:** [42859641](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42859641/) | **PMCID:** [PMC13653512](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC13653512/)
- **Topics:** Antimicrobial Resistance

> PURPOSE: To evaluate the clinical and economic outcomes associated with implementation of an optimized blood culture workflow in intensive care unit (ICU) patients with bloodstream infections.
> 
> PATIENTS AND METHODS: From March 2022 to June 2023, the laboratory implemented a multi-modal workflow optimization including satellite blood culture incubation in the emergency department, upgraded blood culture bottles, rapid MALDI-TOF MS identification, automated rapid antimicrobial susceptibility testing (AST) reporting, and dual AST cards for Gram-negative bacteria. The pre-optimization group included 107 adult ICU patients with positive blood cultures (March 2021 - February 2022). The post-optimization group included 153 patients (September 2023 - August 2024).
> 
> RESULTS: Compared to the final AST report in the pre-optimization group, the rapid AST report in the post-optimization group was 19.6 hours faster (P < 0.001). Total antibiotic use duration was 1.5 days shorter (P = 0.055). Use of two broad-spectrum antibiotics, meropenem (P < 0.001) and tigecycline (P = 0.045), decreased significantly. Hospital length of stay was 5 days shorter (P = 0.006). In unadjusted comparisons, antibiotic and hospitalization costs were reduced by 4871 CNY ([US$ 684], P < 0.001) and 13,336 CNY ([US$ 1873], P = 0.073), respectively.
> 
> CONCLUSION: This multimodal workflow optimization for ICU patients with BSIs, incorporating automated rapid AST reporting and dual-AST-card-enabled expanded antimicrobial panels, shortened laboratory turnaround time. It was associated with earlier opportunities for targeted antimicrobial therapy and suggests potential clinical-economic benefits, alongside possible mitigation of antimicrobial-resistance risk.

### 7. From an emerging pathogen to therapeutic challenges: advances in Achromobacter species, with a focus on A. xylosoxidans and cystic fibrosis.

- **Authors:** Xiang Y, Xiong D, Liu X
- **Journal / Venue:** Front Microbiol
- **Date:** 2026-09-24
- **DOI:** [10.3389/fmicb.2026.1954013](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2026.1954013)
- **PMID:** [42851702](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42851702/) | **PMCID:** [PMC13647113](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC13647113/)
- **Topics:** Antimicrobial Resistance

> Achromobacter spp. are aerobic, non-fermentative, Gram-negative opportunistic pathogens belonging to the family Alcaligenaceae, order Burkholderiales, and are widely distributed in natural environments such as soil and water. In patients with cystic fibrosis (CF), Achromobacter spp. have emerged as increasingly prevalent opportunistic pathogens, with detection rates in respiratory samples rising from approximately 2-3% in the 1990s and early 2000s to 7-10% by the early 2020s in European and North American registries, although reported rates vary considerably by center, age group, and identification method. These bacteria also affect immunocompromised individuals outside the CF population. However, precise diagnosis and treatment of Achromobacter infections remain particularly challenging because of progressive difficulties: inaccurate species-level identification, limited clinical breakpoints for most species, and synergistic multidrug resistance mechanisms. This review synthesizes recent epidemiological data, outbreak investigations, and tentative epidemiological cutoff values (ECOFFs) for A. xylosoxidans established by EUCAST. We also present an integrated framework that links motility loss, biofilm formation, and resistance development to describe within-host adaptive evolution in the CF airway, and we highlight the role of biofilm-mediated tolerance and persister cells in chronic infection. By synthesizing multidimensional evidence, this review aims to provide a comprehensive reference framework for basic research and clinical management of Achromobacter, and to identify priority research directions, including the establishment of standardized identification procedures and the advancement of standardized clinical development of phage therapy.

### 8. Comprehensive genomic characterization of multidrug-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae from North India.

- **Authors:** Bali NK, Siddique AI, Majid U, et al.
- **Journal / Venue:** Front Microbiol
- **Date:** 2026-09-24
- **DOI:** [10.3389/fmicb.2026.1831365](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2026.1831365)
- **PMID:** [42851676](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42851676/) | **PMCID:** [PMC13646146](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC13646146/)
- **Topics:** Antimicrobial Resistance, Metagenomics

> BACKGROUND: Klebsiella pneumoniae is a major cause of healthcare-associated infections and an important contributor to the global burden of antimicrobial resistance. However, comprehensive genomic epidemiological data on multidrug-resistant (MDR) K. pneumoniae from North India remain limited.
> 
> METHODS: Ninety-six non-duplicate clinical K. pneumoniae isolates collected from a tertiary-care hospital in North India during 2022-2024 underwent antimicrobial susceptibility testing and de novo whole-genome sequencing (WGS). Following genomic quality assessment and targeted re-evaluation, 90 genomes were retained for genome-based analyses. MLST and pangenome analyses included 72 and 69 genomes, respectively, according to their respective inclusion criteria. Genome-based analyses included capsular typing, resistome and virulome profiling, plasmid replicon prediction, pangenome analysis, identification of resistance-associated chromosomal mutations, and phylogenomic reconstruction.
> 
> RESULTS: Phenotypic analysis showed carbapenem resistance in 93/96 (96.9%) isolates. Genomic analysis identified 32 sequence types, with ST515 (37.5%; 27/72) as the predominant lineage, while KL62 (60.0%; 54/90) was the most common capsular locus. The predominant carbapenemase gene was bla OXA-232 (75.6%; 68/90), followed by bla NDM-1 (14.4%; 13/90) and bla NDM-5 (8.9%; 8/90). A total of 82 antimicrobial resistance genes spanning 16 antibiotic classes were identified, with fosA (86/90, 95.6%), oqxA (85/90, 94.4%), qnrS1 (75/90, 83.3%), and bla CTX-M-15 (69/90, 76.7%) being the most prevalent. Pangenome analysis of 69 genomes identified 13,720 gene families, indicating extensive accessory genome diversity. Resistance-associated mutations involving ompK35, ompK36, gyrA, parC, ramR, and cirA were identified. Virulence profiling revealed conserved genes for adherence, biofilm formation, secretion systems, and iron acquisition, whereas rmpA and rmpA2 were absent. Phylogenomic analysis demonstrated clustering according to sequence type and capsular locus.
> 
> CONCLUSION: This study provides a comprehensive genomic characterization of MDR K. pneumoniae circulating in a North Indian tertiary care hospital. The predominance of the ST515 and KL62 lineage, together with high prevalence of bla OXA-232, with an extensive accessory genome and diverse resistance determinants, highlights the value of WGS for genomic surveillance of carbapenem-resistant K. pneumoniae and for informing infection prevention and antimicrobial stewardship.

### 10. Wavelet-based whole genome doubling aware single-cell copy number calling.

- **Authors:** Wesley BK, Wos F, Germer S, et al.
- **Journal / Venue:** Nucleic Acids Res
- **Date:** 2026-Sep-22
- **DOI:** [10.1093/nar/gkag960](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkag960)
- **PMID:** [42851130](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42851130/) | **PMCID:** [PMC13650168](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC13650168/)
- **Topics:** Antimicrobial Resistance

> Advances in single-cell whole genome sequencing enable profiling of the copy number state of thousands of cells with minimal sequencing bias across the genome. The Direct Library Preparation+ technique is a whole genome amplification-free single-cell whole genome sequencing method that achieves high throughput by fragmenting each cell's genome and ligating sequencing adapters using a modified Tn5 transposase, and sequencing to <0.1× coverage. Despite recent advances in experimental approaches, data analysis of single-cell whole genome sequencing lags behind and the existing methods are not optimized for the analysis of frozen samples with variable DNA preservation. We demonstrate that Songbird is robust down to very low coverage, adaptable to a variety of genome versions (hg19, hg38, hs.1), and is extensible to other single-cell whole genome sequencing methods that rely on Tn5 tagmentation to fragment the genome.

### 11. When the fungus is susceptible, but the patient is not: reading antifungal susceptibility from bench to bedside.

- **Authors:** Lass-Flörl C, Vahedi-Shahandashti R
- **Journal / Venue:** Lancet Infect Dis
- **Date:** 2026-10-08
- **DOI:** [10.1016/S1473-3099(26)00424-X](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1473-3099(26)00424-X)
- **PMID:** [42849476](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42849476/)
- **Topics:** Antimicrobial Resistance

> Antifungal susceptibility testing is increasingly available and is central to the management of invasive, refractory, recurrent, and breakthrough fungal infections. However, minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) are not self-interpreting results. They measure in vitro antifungal activity under defined laboratory conditions, but clinical outcome is shaped by species identification, method, breakpoint or epidemiological cutoff values, availability, pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic exposure, infection site, fungal burden, source control, biofilm formation, and host immunity. Non-classic fungal drug-response phenotypes, including tolerance, heteroresistance, persistence, adaptive resistance, trailing growth, paradoxical growth, and biofilm-associated survival, can further weaken the link between a susceptible MIC and therapeutic success. In this Personal View, we argue that antifungal susceptibility testing should be understood as a shared clinical-laboratory interface rather than as a standalone laboratory result. The microbiologist makes the MIC interpretable by defining organism, method, interpretive threshold, confidence, and need for confirmation; the clinician makes it clinically useful by integrating exposure, site, source control, toxicity, interactions, and clinical trajectory. Future reports should therefore move from isolated MICs towards structured interpretation, uncertainty flags, and interpretive guidance, while avoiding forced susceptible-resistant categorisation when evidence is incomplete. Such reporting can strengthen stewardship, surveillance, and patient-level decisions across tertiary, routine, and resource-constrained settings, without overstating what MICs can predict alone at the bedside.

### 12. Resistance of Helicobacter pylori to clarithromycin and levofloxacin in patients residing in the VI Region of Chile.

- **Authors:** Estrella L, Mosquera M, Orosco J, et al.
- **Journal / Venue:** Diagn Microbiol Infect Dis
- **Date:** 2026-10-02
- **DOI:** [10.1016/j.diagmicrobio.2026.117684](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.diagmicrobio.2026.117684)
- **PMID:** [42849411](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42849411/)
- **Topics:** Antimicrobial Resistance

> BACKGROUND: In Chile, antibiotic resistance in Helicobacter pylori varies by region, with clarithromycin and levofloxacin showing the highest rates of resistance. Data from rural populations remain scarce, limiting regional treatment decisions.
> 
> OBJECTIVES: This study aims to determine antibiotic resistance rates and identify the most common mutations associated with clarithromycin and levofloxacin resistance in rural patients from the VI Region of Chile.
> 
> METHODS: A prospective cross-sectional analytical study was conducted from 2018 to 2019 at Ricardo Valenzuela Sáez Hospital in Rengo, Chile. DNA was extracted from gastric mucosal tissue on cellulose support obtained during rapid urease test endoscopy in 187 consecutively enrolled, treatment-naïve patients, and antibiotic resistance was detected by mutational analysis using the GenoType HelicoDR commercial kit.
> 
> RESULTS: Resistance rates were 18.7% for clarithromycin and 13.4% for levofloxacin, both exceeding international clinical thresholds of 15% and 10%, respectively. Mutations A2147G and N87K were the most prevalent for each antibiotic. Female sex was significantly associated with levofloxacin resistance (p = 0.037). Cellulose support from the rapid urease test proved effective for DNA extraction and mutation detection.
> 
> CONCLUSIONS: Our results provide the first evidence of resistance to clarithromycin and levofloxacin in a rural area of Chile. The resistance levels observed compromise the use of both antibiotics as empirical therapy in this population. Furthermore, our method detects antibiotic resistance by molecular DNA-based methods using the same sample obtained for the rapid urease test, without requiring an additional biopsy for this purpose; this approach does not extend to phenotypic, culture-based susceptibility testing.

### 13. Efflux reprogramming promotes Staphylococcus aureus persistence with Pseudomonas aeruginosa in cystic fibrosis.

- **Authors:** Truong-Bolduc QC, Wang Y, Alvarez-Carcamo B, et al.
- **Journal / Venue:** Antimicrob Agents Chemother
- **Date:** 2026-10-08
- **DOI:** [10.1128/aac.01070-26](https://doi.org/10.1128/aac.01070-26)
- **PMID:** [42848033](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42848033/)
- **Topics:** Antimicrobial Resistance

> Staphylococcus aureus persists in cystic fibrosis (CF) airways despite in vitro antibiotic susceptibility, suggesting that efflux reprogramming driven by environmental and polymicrobial interactions with Pseudomonas aeruginosa promotes survival in this setting. In this study, we investigated how CF-like conditions and co-infection with P. aeruginosa influence S. aureus physiology and antimicrobial response. Using artificial sputum medium (ASM) and co-culture models, we found that ASM induced a selective transcriptional response characterized by upregulation of the multidrug efflux pump tet38, while other pumps remained minimally expressed. In the presence of P. aeruginosa, S. aureus maintained tet38 expression but additionally induced norB, indicating a redistribution of efflux activity under polymicrobial stress. P. aeruginosa metabolites, including quinolines and phenazines, contributed to this response, and deletion of tet38 or norB significantly reduced S. aureus fitness in ASM and co-culture conditions. Analysis of S. aureus clinical isolates from people with CF revealed a similar hierarchy, with Tet38 and NorB as the predominant efflux pumps, particularly in S. aureus isolates co-recovered with P. aeruginosa. Despite exhibiting only low-level increases in MICs, these isolates demonstrated enhanced survival in ASM. Notably, S. aureus isolates paired with clinical P. aeruginosa strains showed improved survival compared with survival when paired with a P. aeruginosa reference strain, consistent with adaptation toward coexistence. Together, these findings demonstrate that CF-like environmental conditions and polymicrobial interactions drive selective reprogramming of efflux pumps that promote S. aureus persistence without conferring high-level resistance, highlighting a mechanism of antibiotic tolerance not captured by standard susceptibility testing.

### 14. A multi-matrix workflow for isolation, characterization, and biobanking of antimicrobial- resistant bacteria from One Health surveillance samples.

- **Authors:** Bahr A, Heiden SE, Karnatz D, et al.
- **Journal / Venue:** Front Microbiol
- **Date:** 2026-09-23
- **DOI:** [10.3389/fmicb.2026.1855350](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2026.1855350)

- **Topics:** Antimicrobial Resistance

> OBJECTIVES: Standardized protocols for the isolation of antimicrobial-resistant (AMR) bacteria are typically optimized for individual sample types, limiting comparability across One Health surveillance domains. Here, we describe a multi-matrix workflow for the isolation, characterization, and biobanking of AMR bacteria (Enterobacterales, Pseudomonadales and Staphylococcus aureus) from diverse One Health sample types, with the aim of enabling reproducible and comparable AMR surveillance across sectors and settings.
>

> 
> RESULTS APPLICATION AND DEMONSTRATION: Application of the workflow to samples of varying complexity demonstrates its capacity to recover multiple AMR bacterial species and distinct strains across matrices. More complex samples yielded a higher number of isolates, and the picker scheme enabled discrimination of phenotypically and genotypically distinct strains within individual samples, illustrating the protocol's resolution and robustness.
